A good cut is not a costume.

It is the shape your hair falls into on a Tuesday morning, six weeks after the appointment, when nobody is watching.

Words: the salon · Photograph: Pexels · Towngate, Ossett

Most bad haircuts start before the scissors. They start with a picture, a rushed answer, or a stylist who has already decided what the hair should do.

This chair works the other way around. Density, growth pattern and the amount of time you will actually spend styling decide the shape. Then the cut is built to hold it.

Colour gets the same treatment: a plan for the roots, the fade and the next appointment, rather than a shade that only works under salon lights.

The craft

Report · two-minute read

Built to survive the school run.

A haircut that only works when a stylist blows it out is a performance, not a service. The useful version is quieter: a shape that keeps its line when it dries on its own, and grows out without a six-week emergency.

That means cutting for the real head in the chair. Fine hair gets weight removed where it collapses. Curls get shaped dry, where the spring is visible. A grown-out fringe gets a plan rather than a rescue.

“If it will not work at home, it is not the right cut.”The only rule that matters

Colour follows the same logic. Roots, tone and shine are mapped over the next few appointments, so the colour fades into something intentional instead of something tired.

Questions, answered

Desk notes

How do I book?

Contact the salon directly. That is the fastest way to check the diary and confirm a time.

Can I bring a photo?

Yes — but be ready to talk about what you like in it. Shape, length or colour is more useful than a whole look.

Do you take walk-ins?

When the diary allows. Booking ahead is the safe way to get the chair and time you want.

What is the cancellation policy?

Ask when you book. The salon confirms its own notice period and any deposit.
